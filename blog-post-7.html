From Idea to Initiative: How We Started in 3 Weeks

MH
Muhammad Hammad
Technical Lead
April 5, 2026
7 min read

It is now April. Seven weeks ago, this club did not exist. There was no name, no Instagram page, no website, no storage room, no confirmed schools. There was one conversation in a study group at 11pm about a statistic Shema had read.

Here is a relatively honest account of how the next three weeks went.

3Weeks to Launch
7Founding Members
1Functioning Club

Week One: The Conversation

Shema had read that rural schools in Turkey receive significantly fewer educational resources per student than urban schools. He mentioned it offhandedly. Someone said "that's terrible." Someone else said "what can we actually do about it?" That was the question that didn't go away.

By the end of that session, there were six of us who'd said some version of "I'd help with that." İskender joined the next day after Warris told him about it. That was seven.

We didn't have a plan. We had a shared chat, a rough idea (collect supplies, deliver them), and no structure. The first thing we agreed on was to meet once a week and not let it become one of those projects that dissolves into a shared note no one reads.

"I've joined three 'initiatives' at university that went nowhere. The difference this time was that in the first meeting, Warris had already looked up how to register a student club and what the university required. That level of practical thinking made me believe it would actually happen."

— Rahima Noor, Education Year 4

Week Two: The Infrastructure

Week two was the least glamorous part. It was paperwork, logistics, and a lot of decisions that felt small but mattered.

  • Club registration. Warris handled this. The university requires a faculty advisor, a founding member list, and a basic mission statement. We had all three by Tuesday.
  • The name. We spent more time on this than we'd like to admit. "Işık to Villages" won because it was direct, connected to the university, and worked in both Turkish and English contexts.
  • The website. Shema built the first version in a weekend. I helped with the form structure and data handling. Mert designed the visual identity — the logo, the color scheme — in parallel. By Sunday night we had something we could show people.
  • Instagram. Baran set this up and wrote the first three posts. The account had 40 followers by end of week, mostly friends. It was a start.

Week Three: The First Contact

The initiative needed a real school to be real. An Instagram page collecting books for an unnamed future purpose is a project. A confirmed school partnership is a commitment.

Rahima had contacts through her faculty placement program. She reached out to a teacher she'd worked with in Ordu — Mehmet Bey — who connected us to the school administration. The school said yes within a week.

That yes changed everything. Suddenly the supply drive had a recipient. The donations had a destination. The work had a face — twenty-eight students in a classroom in Ordu who would eventually hold books that came from our campus.

By the end of week three, we had a registered club, a website, an Instagram account, a confirmed school, and a plan for our first supply drive. We'd gone from a conversation to a functioning initiative in twenty-one days.

The honest version: it was chaotic, some things slipped, and there are things we'd do differently. But the core worked. And the core was simple: move fast, keep commitments, and don't let the perfect plan stop the good enough one from happening.
