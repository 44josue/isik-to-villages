Meet the Faces Behind Işık To Villages

MK
Mert Kaan
Design Lead
May 5, 2026
6 min read

We often get asked: who are you, really? The Instagram page has a logo and some captions. The website has a mission statement. But behind all of it are seven students at FMV Işık University who decided, at some point this semester, that doing nothing wasn't good enough.

This post is our attempt to introduce ourselves — not as a club, but as people.

The Seven of Us

We come from three different faculties and four different countries. What we share is a campus, a frustration with how little support reaches rural schools in Turkey, and a willingness to do something about it.

  • Shema Josue — Computer Engineering, Year 2. Born in Rwanda, raised in various places. Shema had the original idea after reading about the resource gap between urban and rural schools during an assignment. He built the first version of this website in a weekend.
  • Warris Hasan — Business Administration, Year 3. Warris runs our logistics — drop-off coordination, delivery scheduling, storage. If something needs to move, he makes it move.
  • Mert Kaan — Industrial Design, Year 2. That's me. I handle visual design: posters, social posts, the look and feel of our materials. My first contribution was a poster I made in 40 minutes for the book drive.
  • Baran Yöngel — Psychology, Year 3. Baran handles our relationships — with donors, school teachers, and university administration. He's the person who makes sure we stay connected with the communities we're trying to serve.
  • Rahima Noor — Education, Year 4. Rahima is our bridge to the schools. She knows what teachers actually need, not just what we assume they need. Her faculty placement program has taken her to two rural schools already.
  • İskender Tunç — International Relations, Year 2. İskender handles our partnerships and outreach. The Aydın province connection happened because of a call he made in March.
  • Muhammad Hammad — Computer Engineering, Year 3. Hammad keeps the technical infrastructure running — our online forms, the Drive project, and the backend thinking behind how we collect and distribute donations.

Our Backgrounds

One thing that surprised us when we first started meeting was how different our reasons for joining were. Rahima had seen underfunded classrooms first-hand during her faculty placement. İskender had grown up in a smaller city and remembered what it felt like when your school didn't have enough. Shema had read a statistic and couldn't stop thinking about it.

None of us came to this with the same story. But we all arrived at the same conclusion: this is a solvable problem if people with resources — even modest student-sized resources — decide to act.

"I grew up in Eskişehir, not a village. But I remember the teachers talking about the schools in the smaller districts nearby. They had nothing. And we had more than we needed. That gap was always there."

— İskender Tunç, International Relations Year 2

Why This Specifically

We get this question a lot. Why books and school supplies? Why not tuition support, or infrastructure, or something bigger?

The honest answer: because it's what we can actually do. A student club with no budget can still collect books. We can still fill a van and drive to Ordu. We can still make a difference that a teacher notices on Monday morning when the shelves look different from Friday.

We're not trying to solve the whole problem. We're trying to solve the part of it that's within reach — and then reach a little further each semester.
