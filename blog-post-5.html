Volunteer Spotlight: How Students Are Making a Difference

BY
Baran Yöngel
Community Liaison
April 20, 2026
5 min read

Every organization has people who do more than they signed up for. They're the ones who stay after meetings, pick up the tasks no one claims, and somehow keep the energy from dropping when things get difficult. This post is about three of those people in our club.

I'm not using their last names — I asked their permission to share these profiles, and they all agreed on the condition that I "not make it weird." I'll do my best.

3Spotlights
120+Volunteer Hours
6Events Organized

Spotlight One: The Organizer

Dilnoza is a second-year Business student from Uzbekistan. She joined the club in March after seeing a post on the university notice board. Within two weeks she had rewritten our donation intake process, created a tracking spreadsheet for incoming items, and volunteered to manage the storage room schedule.

She is the reason we know exactly how many items we've collected at any point. Before Dilnoza, we were estimating. After Dilnoza, we have a spreadsheet that updates in real time and sends a summary to the group chat every Sunday.

When I asked her why she put so much time into this, she said: "I'm studying business. This is better practice than any case study."

"I didn't think about it as volunteering. I thought about it as: there's a problem that needs a system, and I know how to build systems. So I built one."

— Dilnoza, Business Administration Year 2

Spotlight Two: The Connector

Emre is a third-year Engineering student who grew up in Trabzon. He has an uncle who teaches in a village school near the Black Sea coast, which is how he first understood what "underfunded" actually looks like in practice.

Emre has driven the van on three separate collection trips, coordinated the handoff with the school in Ordu, and spent his own money fixing a box of books that arrived water-damaged before we could donate them. He didn't tell anyone about the repair — we only found out because another member noticed the restored books at the school and asked.

He shrugged when we brought it up. "They were good books."

Spotlight Three: The Communicator

Zehra is a first-year Psychology student who joined the club three weeks ago. In that time she has written captions for twelve Instagram posts, responded to every DM our account has received, and personally followed up with four donors who hadn't heard back about their contribution.

She's the reason our Instagram presence feels human. Before Zehra, our posts were informational. After Zehra, they feel like they're from people who care — because they are, and now the writing reflects that.

She told me she joined because she wanted to feel like her time at university was about something. I think she's finding that.
